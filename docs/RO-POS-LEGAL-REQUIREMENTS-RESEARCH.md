# Romania: Legal Prerequisites for a POS Sale — Research (2026)

Research question: for a small café/restaurant (1–3 locations) in Romania, what does the law actually require *before* the business can legally ring up a sale through a POS system? This informs how strict FranchiseTech's signup/onboarding gate should be (CUI check, e-Factura assumptions, cash-register nag) before letting an owner record their first sale.

Method: primary sources prioritized — ANAF (anaf.ro / static.anaf.ro), Ministry of Finance (mfinante.gov.ro), Portal Legislativ (legislatie.just.ro), ONRC (onrc.ro). Where a primary document could not be fetched directly (several `legislatie.just.ro` and `mfinante.gov.ro` pages timed out / reset during this research), the underlying **statutory text was still obtained**, either from an ANAF-hosted copy of the same law/PDF or from the Ministry of Finance's own republished PDF, and is quoted directly below. Pure accounting/law-firm blog claims are marked **[secondary]** and are only used to corroborate, never as the sole basis for a legal claim.

---

## 1. Is CUI + ANAF registration a legal prerequisite to selling anything, or just onboarding convenience?

**Direct answer: Yes, it is a hard legal prerequisite — but not for the reason the question implies.** A Romanian company cannot legally exist, let alone sell anything, before it is registered in the Trade Registry (registrul comerțului). CUI assignment happens automatically and inseparably *as part of* that same registration — it is not a downstream, skippable step. Separately, **VAT registration** (a different, later, threshold-based step) is *not* required to make sales — a non‑VAT‑registered company can legally sell (just without charging VAT).

**Supporting sources:**

- **Legea nr. 31/1990 (Legea societăților), Art. 41 alin. (1):** *"Societatea comercială este persoană juridică de la data înmatriculării în registrul comerțului."* — "The company is a legal person from the date of its registration in the Trade Registry." Before that date, there is no legal entity capable of contracting, invoicing, or owning a cash register at all.
  Source: ONRC (National Trade Registry Office) official consolidated text — https://www.onrc.ro/documente/legislatie/noi/legea_nr_31_modif_26.10.2011.pdf (Art. 40–41). This provision is foundational company law and is confirmed unchanged in current (2026) consolidations on secondary legal-database mirrors **[secondary corroboration]**: https://lege5.ro/gratuit/gy4diobx/legea-societatilor-nr-31-1990

- **Legea nr. 265/2022 privind registrul comerțului**, Secțiunea a 10-a ("Atribuirea codului unic de înregistrare și înregistrarea fiscală a profesioniștilor care au obligația înregistrării în registrul comerțului"):
  - **Art. 125:** *"Solicitarea înregistrării fiscale se face prin depunerea cererii de înregistrare la biroul unic din cadrul oficiului registrului comerțului de pe lângă tribunal, iar atribuirea codului unic de înregistrare de către Ministerul Finanțelor – ANAF este condiționată de admiterea cererii de înregistrare în registrul comerțului."* — Fiscal registration is filed at the same one-stop-shop window as trade-registry registration, and **ANAF's CUI assignment is conditional on the trade-registry application being admitted.**
  - **Art. 127 alin. (1):** *"Certificatul de înregistrare conținând codul unic de înregistrare este documentul care atestă că persoana juridică a dobândit personalitate juridică, respectiv că profesioniștii au fost luați în evidența oficiului registrului comerțului de pe lângă tribunal și în evidența organului fiscal."* — The registration certificate bearing the CUI **is the very document that proves the company has acquired legal personality** and has simultaneously been recorded with both the Trade Registry and ANAF.
  Source: official text hosted by ANAF — https://static.anaf.ro/static/10/Anaf/legislatie/L_265_2022.pdf (fetched and read directly during this research).

- **VAT registration is a separate, non-gating matter.** Below the small-enterprise exemption threshold, a business can legally sell and issue receipts/invoices without being VAT-registered (it simply doesn't charge VAT). The current threshold is reported at **395,000 lei** annual turnover (raised from 300,000 lei), effective **1 September 2025**, under EU Directive 2020/285's transposition into the Fiscal Code (Art. 310). I confirmed this turnover-threshold regime exists in the current Fiscal Code text, but I could **not** pin down with full certainty, inside this research pass, the single exact normative act that raised the number to 395,000 lei — secondary sources consistently cite it (**[secondary]**: https://bono.ro/blog/plafon-tva-srl, https://infotva.manager.ro/articole/studii-de-caz/plafon-tva-de-395000-lei-in-2026-ce-venituri-se-iau-in-calcul-studiu-de-caz-util-24526.html, https://www.contzilla.ro/plafoane-fiscale-2026-micro-tva-intrastat/), and the Ministry of Finance itself published a press release with this exact figure in its title (https://mfinante.gov.ro/en/acasa/-/asset_publisher/uwgr/content/suport-fiscal-pentru-c3-aentreprinderile-mici-prin-modific-c4-83ri-de-legisla-c8-9bie-cre-c8-99te-plafonul-de-scutire-de-tva-de-la-300.000-lei-la-395.000-lei) — but the mfinante.gov.ro page itself could not be fetched in this session (connection reset on every attempt), so treat the *exact figure and date* as secondary-corroborated, not independently verified against the raw law text.

**Confidence: High** on the core claim (CUI/legal-personality is a hard gate; VAT registration is not). **Medium** on the precise current VAT threshold figure/date (well-corroborated by secondary sources and an MF press-release title, but not independently confirmed against primary statutory text in this pass).

## 2. Is e-Factura mandatory for a café's day-to-day retail/POS sales (a walk-in coffee), or only for B2B/requested invoices?

**Direct answer: e-Factura's B2C mandate does *not* apply to ordinary walk-in POS sales documented by a fiscal receipt (bon fiscal).** It applies when the business issues an actual *invoice* (factură) to a consumer — which typically only happens when the customer asks for one and identifies themselves (e.g., via CNP). A simplified invoice / qualifying fiscal receipt is explicitly excepted.

**Supporting sources — primary statutory text, quoted directly:**

- **OUG nr. 120/2021, Art. 2 alin. (1) lit. n¹)** (introduced by OUG nr. 69/2024): defines the "B2C relation" as a transaction between a Romania-established taxable economic operator and a non-taxable person (i.e., a consumer).
- **Art. 10¹ alin. (2)** (introduced by OUG nr. 69/2024): *"Începând cu data de 1 ianuarie 2025, operatorii economici ... au obligația să transmită facturile emise în sistemul național privind factura electronică RO e-Factura [pentru B2C]. **Fac excepție facturile simplificate emise conform art. 319 alin. (12) din Legea nr. 227/2015**, cu modificările și completările ulterioare."* — From 1 January 2025, businesses must transmit B2C **invoices** through RO e-Factura — but **simplified invoices under Art. 319(12) of the Fiscal Code are explicitly excepted.**
- **Art. 10¹ alin. (3):** *"Livrările de bunuri/Prestările de servicii efectuate către o persoană fizică care se identifică în relația cu furnizorul/prestatorul prin codul numeric personal se consideră efectuate în relația B2C."* — The B2C e-Factura duty is anchored to a delivery where the individual **identifies themselves via their CNP** — i.e., where a proper personalized invoice is actually issued, not an anonymous over-the-counter cash-register sale.
- Fiscal receipts (bonuri fiscale) that meet the value/format conditions of a "simplified invoice" under Fiscal Code Art. 319(12) (broadly: value including VAT up to the equivalent of 100 EUR) are treated as simplified invoices and are therefore covered by the exception above — this is stated directly in the source below and corroborated by several Romanian tax-practice sources **[secondary]**: https://www.fiscalitatea.ro/facturi-simplificate-exista-obligatia-transmiterii-lor-in-e-factura-23721/, https://www.portalpfa.ro/articole/legislatie-1/bon-fiscal-cu-rol-de-factura-simplificata-conditii-pentru-deducere-cheltuieli-2785.html.

  Source for the quoted statutory text: ANAF's own regional taxpayer-assistance office (DGRFP Brașov) explanatory bulletin reproducing OUG 69/2024's exact before/after article text — https://static.anaf.ro/static/10/Brasov/Brasov/oug_69_e_factura.pdf. OUG nr. 69/2024 was published in **Monitorul Oficial nr. 582 din 21.06.2024**.

**Timeline (confirmed from the same primary/ANAF text plus MF/CECCAR corroboration):**
- 1 Jul 2024 – 31 Dec 2024: B2C transmission via RO e-Factura optional.
- **1 January 2025: mandatory** for B2C invoices (not fiscal receipts) as defined above.
- Sanctions for non-transmission apply from **1 July 2025** (6-month grace period on penalties only) — fines tiered 1,000–2,500 lei (small taxpayers/individuals) up to 5,000–10,000 lei (large taxpayers) **[primary, same ANAF document, quoting the amended Legea nr. 296/2023 Art. 13² alin. (3)]**.
- Associations/foundations/political parties/cults not VAT-registered, and individual farmers under the special regime, were exempted until 30 June 2025 **[primary, same source]**.

**Separate, evolving track — do not confuse with e-Factura:** OUG 69/2024 also amended OUG 28/1999 (the cash-register law) to require a **QR code** on every fiscal receipt (new Art. 4 alin. (2¹) of OUG 28/1999), in force since **21 June 2024**, with up to a **2-year derogation** (i.e., roughly to mid-2026) for cash-register models that fail ICI București technical testing for QR printing — this is primary-sourced from the same ANAF Brașov document. Multiple secondary sources (StartupCafe, ValidSoftware, Găzarul) additionally report a **draft/finalized Government Decision requiring fuller ANAF data transmission from cash registers by 1 November 2026** — a further modernization of the *bon fiscal*, not an extension of e-Factura to retail sales. I could not independently verify this specific 1 Nov 2026 date and its legal status (draft HG vs. finalized) against a primary source in this pass, because mfinante.gov.ro connections repeatedly failed — **flag this date as secondary/unconfirmed**, though the direction (cash registers gaining more ANAF connectivity over time) is consistent with the confirmed primary trend.

**Confidence: High** that ordinary POS/bon-fiscal retail sales are excluded from mandatory e-Factura today, and that e-Factura B2C targets requested/personalized invoices, not anonymous retail transactions. **Medium** on the exact 100 EUR simplified-invoice threshold figure (well corroborated but not fetched from the raw Fiscal Code text directly). **Low/unconfirmed** on the specific "1 November 2026" cash-register data-transmission deadline.

## 3. Is a fiscal cash register required from the very first sale, or is there a grace period?

**Direct answer: No grace period.** The obligation to use a fiscal electronic cash register (casă de marcat fiscală) attaches from the very first day of commercial activity at each location — the law states this explicitly.

**Supporting sources — primary statutory text, quoted directly from the Ministry of Finance's own republished text of OUG nr. 28/1999** (fetched directly: https://mfinante.gov.ro/documents/35673/217581/oug_28_1999.pdf — consolidated text in force as of 10 September 2018; confirmed that the later OUG 69/2024 only amended receipt-content/QR-code provisions (Art. 4) and did not touch Art. 1, 6, 10 or 11 discussed below):

- **Art. 1 alin. (1):** *"Operatorii economici care încasează, integral sau parțial, cu numerar sau prin utilizarea cardurilor de credit/debit sau a substitutelor de numerar contravaloarea bunurilor livrate cu amănuntul, precum și a prestărilor de servicii efectuate direct către populație sunt obligați să utilizeze aparate de marcat electronice fiscale."* — Any business taking cash or card payment for retail goods or services sold directly to consumers must use a fiscal cash register. A café selling coffee to walk-in customers falls squarely inside this definition (and outside the narrow exception list in Art. 2, which covers things like newspaper vendors, taxi-style public transport, vending machines, home-delivery-only mail order, etc. — not cafés/restaurants).
- **Art. 6:** *"Obligația operatorilor economici prevăzuți la art. 1 de a utiliza aparate de marcat electronice fiscale ia naștere de la data începerii activităților comerciale desfășurate în fiecare locație."* — **The obligation arises from the date commercial activities begin at each location.** There is no stated grace period (e.g., no "30 days to get compliant") — the duty is triggered by trading, not by a separate registration deadline.
- **Art. 10 lit. e)** makes it a contravention (regulatory offense) for an operator to fail to equip itself with a fiscal cash register purchased from an authorized distributor.
- **Art. 11 alin. (1) lit. d):** that specific contravention is punished with a fine of **10,000–20,000 lei**, plus **confiscation of the unjustified sums** and **suspension of the business's activity at that sales point until it equips itself with a fiscal cash register** and pays the fine.
- **Art. 10 lit. c) / Art. 11 alin. (1) lit. e):** separately, failing to *issue* a fiscal receipt for a sale (even if a cash register is present) is its own contravention, with fines scaled to the undocumented amount (from a warning up to 20,000 lei) plus confiscation.

**Practical fiscalization mechanics** (context, not a separate legal gate on selling): once a business buys a cash register, it must be "fiscalized" — a technician activates the fiscal memory and the business obtains a NUI (unique installation number) from ANAF via Form C801 submitted through the business's SPV account, then registers the device with the local tax administration. This is a hardware/process step, not a separate waiting period the law grants before the Art. 1/6 obligation applies — **[secondary, but consistent with the primary Art. 1/6/10/11 text above]**: https://sistemefiscale.ro/fiscalizare-casa-de-marcat-ghid-complet-si-preturi/, https://amef.ro/fiscalizare/, and ANAF's own installation guide (2018) at https://static.anaf.ro/static/21/Anaf/Ghid_case_marcat_05102018.pdf.

**Confidence: High.** Art. 1, Art. 6, Art. 10, and Art. 11 were read directly from the Ministry of Finance's own republished PDF of the law, and the "no grace period" reading of Art. 6 is unambiguous on its face.

## Product implication

For FranchiseTech's signup/onboarding flow, these three facts point to different levels of strictness:

1. **CUI existence check — should be a hard gate, but as a sanity/fraud check, not a "new business isn't ready yet" block.** By the time any Romanian business can legally exist to sign up at all, it already has a CUI (company registration and CUI assignment are the same event under Legea 265/2022, Art. 125–127). So requiring a valid, ANAF-recognized CUI before allowing a first recorded sale isn't blocking a real business from operating sooner than the law allows — it's just verifying the business is real. **Do not,** however, gate onboarding on **VAT registration** — that's a separate, threshold-based status a legitimate, currently-operating café may simply not have yet, and the law does not require it to sell.

2. **e-Factura should not be forced into the POS "ring up a sale" path.** For a café's normal walk-in transactions, the legally correct document is the fiscal receipt (bon fiscal), not an e-Factura submission — e-Factura B2C only applies when a customer explicitly wants a personalized invoice (CNP-identified). Building e-Factura transmission as a blocking step on every POS sale would be over-engineering relative to the actual legal requirement; it should instead be an optional/secondary flow triggered only when the customer requests an invoice.

3. **Fiscal cash register connectivity is the one area where "day one" really does mean day one.** Unlike CUI (already resolved by the time the business exists) or e-Factura (not triggered by ordinary POS sales at all), OUG 28/1999 Art. 6 makes the fiscal-cash-register obligation bite from the first day of trading at each location, with real penalties (10,000–20,000 lei plus forced suspension) for non-compliance. If FranchiseTech's POS module is meant to be the fiscal record of sale (rather than a pre-fiscal internal tool sitting alongside a separate certified cash register), this is the requirement to take most seriously in the product's compliance posture — worth confirming, as a follow-up, exactly how FranchiseTech's own FiscalNet integration satisfies Art. 3/Art. 4 (fiscal memory, bon fiscal content, QR code) so the "first sale" recorded in-app is also a legally valid fiscal transaction, not just an internal log entry.

**Bottom line for the gate:** CUI validity — yes, gate it (it's already true for any real business). VAT-registration status — no, do not gate on it. e-Factura — not relevant to gating a POS sale at all. Fiscal cash register compliance — this is the actual legal pressure point on "can this business legally ring up a sale," and deserves more product attention than the CUI-lookup step currently gets.
